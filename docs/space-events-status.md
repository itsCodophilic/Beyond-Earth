- **The trip (round 91):** streaks of starlight in every star's own colour, with faint clouds of glowing gas drifting past between them. The readout gives the speed and the trip's time on Earth and on board. The "faster than light" and "Back home" wording stays gone.

### The kinds of nova (each its own event)

- **What is a nova? (new, round 91; first in the list from round 92).** Her four opening paragraphs, split off the classical nova. A "new star" flares in the real sky; then a nova, a supernova and a kilonova, one after another.

- **A classical nova: Nova Cygni 1975.** V1500 Cygni: a white dwarf feeding from a red dwarf, its hydrogen layer igniting and blown off. The white dwarf survives.
- **A recurrent nova: RS Ophiuchi.** A heavy white dwarf beside a red giant, erupting four times on screen (1933 to 2021), with its eruption record charted. *Round 91:* the chart's year labels no longer overlap.
- **A micronova: TV Columbae.** A magnetic white dwarf with gas pouring down its field lines onto the poles, and a burst on one pole only, a few hours long. *Round 91:* the gas now comes from the stream's side, along the field lines to the poles.
- **A slow symbiotic nova: RR Telescopii.** A pulsating red giant's wind feeding a white dwarf. The flare-up of 1944 has burned for decades, charted 1930–2020. *Round 91:* the labels no longer overlap.
- **What a nova looks like now (round 90).** The donor star has a real surface: a red dwarf's fine cells, or a red giant's few huge ones. The blast lights the whole screen, blue-white, then orange. The gas thrown off is an olive- or peanut-shaped shell drawn out along the poles, with bright knots, cooling from blue-white to red and glowing faint green late on.
- **A Type Ia supernova: Tycho's star, 1572.** A white dwarf gains mass, the counter creeps from 1.30 to 1.40 Suns, and it detonates. Nothing is left; its companion is flung away. *Round 91:* it ends on the remnant rebuilt in 3D from your Chandra picture.
- **A core-collapse supernova: Cassiopeia A.** *The round-1 "Supernova", redesigned.* A yellow supergiant (Cas A's star had lost most of its hydrogen). It is cut open to show the onion of shells, which churn and the iron core falls in. Neutrinos go out first, then the blast lights the whole screen. The camera then goes in close to the neutron star: hot and quiet, with no jets, as Chandra sees it. *Round 92:* it ends on your Webb picture of Cassiopeia A, rebuilt in 3D.
- **Supernova stars (round 90).** Each looks like its real kind:
  - the Crab's: a red supergiant, huge cells, wrapped in dust;
  - 1987A's: a blue supergiant;
  - Cas A's: a yellow supergiant;
  - the hypernova's and the BOAT's: Wolf-Rayet stars, stripped and blue-white, in their own wind.
  All flicker in their last years. The cut-open view shows flat faces coloured by depth, from a red envelope to a blue-white iron core; a stripped star has helium outside, not hydrogen.
- **A kilonova: GW170817.** *The round-1 "Kilonova", redesigned.* Two neutron stars spiral in, with ripples spreading. They merge, a jet fires (the gamma-ray burst), and the debris glows blue, then red. *Round 91, after your picture:*
  - cracked blue-hot crusts, pulled long just before they touch;
  - a hot merged star for a moment, then a torus of debris;
  - the jet as gas.
- **A hypernova: SN 1998bw.** A stripped, fast-spinning star collapses to a black hole, two jets break out, and the star is blown apart with ten times the energy. *Round 91:* the jets are flowing gas, not cones, and the camera then closes on the black hole.
- **A luminous red nova: V838 Monocerotis.** Two stars merge and swell into a red giant, and the flash lights old dust shell by shell (the light echo). *Round 91:* the dust is your Hubble picture rebuilt in 3D, lit as the flash reaches it.
