This is Metis, Jupiter's innermost moon. It races round in about seven hours — faster than Jupiter itself spins — right inside the main ring.

This is Adrastea, one of Jupiter's smallest moons, only about sixteen kilometres across. Dust knocked off it helps feed Jupiter's main ring.

This is Thebe, a small inner moon of Jupiter. Dust from it spreads out into the faintest of Jupiter's rings.

This is Elara, one of the larger moons in Jupiter's Himalia family — a captured rock, far out from the planet.

This is Pasiphae, leader of a family of Jupiter's moons that all go round backwards — probably pieces of one captured asteroid.

This is Sinope, one of Jupiter's far-out moons. It goes round backwards, taking about two years for each lap.

This is Carme, which gives its name to the largest family of Jupiter's moons — dozens of small rocks, all going round backwards together.

This is Ananke, leader of a family of small moons that circle Jupiter backwards — all probably pieces of one broken rock.

This is Themisto, a small moon of Jupiter that was found in nineteen seventy-five, then lost for twenty-five years, before it was found again.

This is Valetudo, a tiny moon that goes round Jupiter forwards while its neighbours go backwards — so one day it may crash into one of them.

This is Prometheus, a shepherd moon. Each time it passes, it pulls streamers of ice out of Saturn's F ring.

This is Pandora, the outer shepherd of Saturn's F ring, working with Prometheus to help keep the ring narrow.

This is Atlas, a little moon just outside Saturn's A ring, with a ridge around its middle that makes it look like a flying saucer.

This is Telesto, one of the travelling companions of the moon Tethys. It rides sixty degrees ahead of Tethys, on the very same orbit.

This is Calypso, Tethys's other companion. It trails sixty degrees behind Tethys, on the very same orbit.

This is Helene, a small moon that rides sixty degrees ahead of Dione, sharing its orbit.

This is Polydeuces, a tiny moon that trails sixty degrees behind Dione, sharing its orbit.

This is Methone, a tiny moon of Saturn so smooth that it looks like an egg.

This is Pallene, a tiny moon of Saturn that leaves a faint ring of dust along its orbit.

This is Anthe, one of the smallest named moons of Saturn, only about two kilometres across.

This is Aegaeon, a moonlet less than a kilometre across, hidden inside a bright arc of Saturn's G ring.
