This is the Liberté arc, one of the bright clumps in Neptune's Adams ring. Arcs like this should spread out all round the ring — something, probably the little moon Galatea, keeps them bunched.

This is the Egalité arc, the brightest part of Neptune's Adams ring — a clump of dust that refuses to spread out.

This is the Fraternité arc, another of the bright clumps of dust in Neptune's Adams ring.

This is the Courage arc, the faintest of the clumps in Neptune's Adams ring — and one that has been fading since Voyager 2 saw it.

Haumea's ring was the first ever found around a world beyond Neptune, in twenty seventeen, when Haumea passed in front of a star.

This is Haumea's ring, narrow and icy. It goes round exactly once for every three spins of Haumea.

Quaoar has two rings, and both break the rules: they circle far beyond the distance where rings should clump together into moons.

This is Quaoar's inner ring, narrow and faint, found in twenty twenty-three.

This is Quaoar's outer ring — more than three times farther out than a ring should be able to survive. By the usual rules, it should have clumped together into a moon.

Chariklo was the first thing that is not a planet found to have rings — two narrow bands, discovered in twenty thirteen, when it passed in front of a star.

This is Oiapoque, Chariklo's inner ring — the first ring ever found around something that is not a planet. It is named after a river at Brazil's northern border.

This is Chuí, Chariklo's fainter outer ring, named after a river at Brazil's southern border.

Chiron may have rings of its own — material seen again and again as it passes in front of stars, perhaps gathered from the dust it throws off when it flares up like a comet.

This is Chiron's inner ring — seen in some passages in front of stars, but not in others, as if it comes and goes.

This is Chiron's main ring, the one seen again and again since twenty eleven.

This is Chiron's outer ring, the farthest of the three.

This is not a ring but a haze — a broad disc of material around Chiron, perhaps dust thrown off when it flares up like a comet.
