This is Ceres, the largest thing in the asteroid belt, and the only dwarf planet there. Bright patches of salt shine on its surface — left behind by salty water that rose up from below.

This is Pluto. Once the ninth planet, now the most famous dwarf planet of all. It has a giant heart-shaped glacier of frozen nitrogen — and a moon so big that the two of them swing around each other.

This is Eris, almost exactly Pluto's size, but heavier. Its discovery started the great debate that ended with Pluto no longer being called a planet.

This is Haumea, the egg-shaped dwarf planet. It spins so fast — once in under four hours — that it has stretched itself out. It was also the first world beyond Neptune found to have a ring.

This is Makemake, a bright dwarf planet beyond Neptune, coated in frozen methane. It was found just after Easter, and named after the creator god of Easter Island.

This is Gonggong, one of the biggest and reddest worlds beyond Neptune, named after a Chinese water god. One trip around the Sun takes it about five hundred and fifty years.

This is Quaoar, a world beyond Neptune with a puzzle around it: a ring sitting far outside the distance where a ring should survive. By the usual rules, it should have clumped together into a moon.

This is Orcus, sometimes called the anti-Pluto. It moves in the same rhythm with Neptune as Pluto does — but always on the opposite side of the Sun.

This is Sedna, one of the most distant worlds we know. Its long, stretched orbit takes more than eleven thousand years — and even at its closest, it never comes near Neptune.

This is Ixion, a large icy world that keeps time with Neptune — two of its laps for every three of Neptune's, the same beat as Pluto.

This is Salacia, a dark, icy world in the Kuiper Belt with a moon of its own, Actaea. It is named after the Roman goddess of the salt sea.

This is Varuna, a big Kuiper Belt world that turns once every six hours — fast enough to squash itself into an egg shape.

This is Varda, named after the queen of the stars in Tolkien's legends. Its moon, Ilmarë, is named after one of her spirits, and the two circle each other far beyond Neptune.
