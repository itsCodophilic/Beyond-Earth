Jupiter has rings too — faint, dark and dusty, nothing like Saturn's. They were only discovered in nineteen seventy-nine, by Voyager 1. They are made of dust, knocked off Jupiter's small inner moons by tiny meteorites.

This is the halo ring, the innermost part of Jupiter's rings — a thick, faint cloud of fine dust, lifted above and below the ring by Jupiter's magnetic field.

This is Jupiter's main ring: narrow, and remarkably thin, fed by dust blasted off the little moons Adrastea and Metis.

This is the Amalthea gossamer ring, a very faint, broad sheet of dust, supplied by the moon Amalthea, and reaching out to its orbit.

This is the Thebe gossamer ring — the faintest and farthest of Jupiter's rings, fed by the little moon Thebe.

Saturn's rings are the grandest in the Solar System — more than two hundred and seventy thousand kilometres across, yet in most places only about ten metres thick. They are made almost entirely of water ice, from specks of dust to chunks as big as houses.

This is the D ring, Saturn's faint innermost ring — so sparse that its icy grains barely ever touch.

This is the C ring, a broad, see-through part of Saturn's main rings, laced with narrow ringlets and gaps.

This is the B ring — the brightest, widest and densest of all Saturn's rings. In places it is so packed with ice that almost no light gets through.

This is the A ring, the outermost of Saturn's three bright rings. Two little moons, Pan and Daphnis, carve the dark Encke and Keeler gaps inside it.

This is the F ring, narrow and knotted, marking the edge of Saturn's main rings. Two tiny shepherd moons, Prometheus and Pandora, help keep it in line.

This is the G ring, faint and dusty. A tiny moon, Aegaeon, hides inside a bright arc near its inner edge.

This is the E ring — an enormous, misty cloud of tiny ice grains, sprayed out by the geysers of Enceladus.

Uranus has thirteen known rings — narrow, dark and tightly packed. They were found in nineteen seventy-seven, when Uranus passed in front of a star, and the starlight blinked off and on before the planet itself arrived.
