This is Hiisi, Lempo's close partner. The two circle each other, while a third world, Paha, circles them both.

This is Paha, the outer companion in the Lempo triple, circling the close pair from far out.

This is Menoetius, the partner of Patroclus — two almost equal worlds circling each other as they trail behind Jupiter. The Lucy spacecraft will visit them in twenty thirty-three.

This is Alexhelios, one of the two little moons of the dog-bone asteroid Kleopatra, named after Cleopatra's son.

This is Cleoselene, Kleopatra's other little moon, named after Cleopatra's daughter.

This is Squannit, the little moon of the near-Earth asteroid Moshup — once known simply as Beta.

This is Skamandrios, the small moon of Hektor, the largest of Jupiter's Trojans.

This is Queta, the tiny moon of Eurybates, found by the Hubble telescope ahead of the Lucy spacecraft's visit.

This is Shaun, the little moon of the Trojan Polymele, found when the pair passed in front of a star in twenty twenty-two.

This is Aten, the first asteroid found with a year shorter than Earth's. The whole Aten family is named after it.

This is Apollo, the asteroid that gives its name to the largest family of near-Earth rocks. It was found in nineteen thirty-two, and it has a small moon.

This is Atira, the first asteroid found that stays inside Earth's orbit the whole way round — and it has a moon of its own.

This is Aylo-chaxnim, the first asteroid ever found that stays inside the orbit of Venus the whole way round. Its name comes from the Luiseño people, and means Venus girl.

This is Icarus, an asteroid that swings closer to the Sun than Mercury, scorching hot, and then out past Mars.

This is Geographos, one of the most stretched-out asteroids known — nearly three times longer than it is wide.

This is Castalia, the first asteroid ever imaged by radar. It turned out to be two lumps, stuck together.

This is Nereus, a small, shiny asteroid — one of the easiest places in the Solar System for a spacecraft to reach from Earth.

This is Florence, one of the largest asteroids to pass near Earth, with two tiny moons of its own.

This is Hathor, a small, quick asteroid whose year is shorter than ours. It spins once every three and a half hours.

This is Eureka, the first Trojan of Mars ever found — and the parent of a little family of fragments that share its spot.
