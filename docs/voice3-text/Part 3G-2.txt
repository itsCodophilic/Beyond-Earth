This is Comet Hartley 2, small and peanut-shaped. A spacecraft flew past it in twenty ten, and found it blasting out jets of gas and chunks of ice.

This is Don Quixote. It looks like an asteroid that comes near Earth — but in two thousand and nine, a space telescope caught it breathing out gas. It is a sleeping comet.

This is Puck, the largest of Uranus's small inner moons, discovered by Voyager 2 in nineteen eighty-five.

This is Galatea, a little moon of Neptune. Its gravity gathers Neptune's outermost ring into bright clumps, called arcs.

This is Larissa, a small, battered moon of Neptune, very slowly spiralling in towards the planet.

This is Vanth, the large moon of Orcus — named after an Etruscan spirit who guided souls into the underworld.

This is Hiiaka, the larger of Haumea's two moons, named after a Hawaiian goddess — one of Haumea's daughters.

This is Namaka, the smaller of Haumea's two moons, named after a Hawaiian water spirit — another of Haumea's daughters.

This is the little dark moon of Makemake, found by the Hubble telescope in twenty sixteen, hiding in its parent's glare.

This is Huya, an icy world that keeps time with Neptune just as Pluto does, with a moon of its own. It is named after the rain spirit of the Wayuu people of South America.

This is Máni, about eight hundred kilometres across — one of the largest worlds beyond Neptune that no spacecraft has seen. It is named after the Moon, in Norse legend.

This is Chaos, a large world in the Kuiper Belt, named after the shapeless void that came before everything else, in Greek myth.

This is Dziewanna, an icy world on a long, tilted orbit, named after a Slavic goddess of spring and the wild.

This is DeeDee. When it was found, it was ninety-two times farther from the Sun than Earth — so far away that it was nicknamed DeeDee, for Distant Dwarf.

This is Leleākūhonua, nicknamed the Goblin. Its orbit stretches out more than two thousand times Earth's distance from the Sun — one of the strange paths that make some astronomers wonder whether a hidden ninth planet is out there.

This is Sila and Nunam, two almost equal icy worlds circling each other. For a few years, seen from Earth, they took turns passing in front of one another.

This is Lempo — not one world but three: a close pair circling each other, and a third companion farther out.
