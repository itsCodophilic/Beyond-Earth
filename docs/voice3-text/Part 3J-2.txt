Neptune's rings are faint and dark, and named after the astronomers who found the planet. The outermost, Adams, holds bright clumps called arcs — which should have smeared out long ago, but haven't.

This is the Galle ring, Neptune's innermost — a faint, broad band of dark dust, named after the astronomer who first saw Neptune.

This is the Le Verrier ring, a narrow, dark ring, named after the mathematician who predicted where Neptune would be found.

This is the Lassell ring, a broad, faint sheet of dust, named after the astronomer who discovered Triton.

This is the Arago ring, a faint ringlet at the outer edge of the Lassell ring.

This is the Adams ring, Neptune's outermost, named after one of the two mathematicians who predicted Neptune. It holds bright arcs of dust that stay bunched together.
