This is Ganymede, the largest moon in the Solar System — bigger than the planet Mercury. It is the only moon with a magnetic field of its own, and it may hide an ocean beneath its ice.

This is Titan, Saturn's largest moon, and the second largest in the Solar System. It is the only moon with a thick atmosphere — and the only place besides Earth with lakes on its surface. Lakes of liquid methane.

This is Callisto, the third largest moon, and the most cratered world we know. Its surface has barely changed in four billion years — a perfect record of the early Solar System.

This is Io, the fourth largest moon, and the most volcanic world in the Solar System. Jupiter squeezes it like a stress ball, and the heat bursts out through hundreds of volcanoes.

This is Europa, the sixth largest moon. Under its shell of ice lies a salty ocean holding more water than all of Earth's oceans — one of the best places to look for life beyond Earth.

This is Triton, Neptune's giant moon, and the seventh largest in the Solar System. It orbits backwards, so it was probably captured — and it has geysers that blow dark plumes kilometres into the sky.

This is Titania, Uranus's largest moon, and the eighth largest in the Solar System — cut by canyons hundreds of kilometres long.

This is Rhea, Saturn's second largest moon, and the ninth largest in the Solar System — a cold, cratered ball made mostly of ice.

This is Oberon, the outermost of Uranus's big moons, and the tenth largest in the Solar System — old, dark and covered in craters.

This is Iapetus, the two-faced moon. One side is as dark as coal, the other as bright as snow — and a ridge of mountains runs around its middle, like the seam of a walnut.

This is Charon, Pluto's great moon, half Pluto's size. The two always face each other, locked together like a pair of dancers.

This is Umbriel, the darkest of Uranus's big moons — quiet and ancient, with one bright ring-shaped mark on its face.

This is Ariel, the brightest of Uranus's big moons, criss-crossed by long valleys from a time when its surface was still young.

This is Dione, a moon of Saturn streaked with bright cliffs of ice, hundreds of metres tall, where its frozen crust has cracked.

This is Tethys, a moon made almost entirely of water ice, with a canyon that runs three-quarters of the way around it.
