# The populations we have not built

Every remaining group of Solar System objects, with what is actually in it and
how many. Counts are **approximate and dated — September 2026** — because
almost all of them go up every month. That lesson is already recorded in
`beyond-earth-scientific-corrections.md`: never hard-code a timeless total.

Read alongside `not-covered.md`, which says *why* things are absent.
This one says *what* is out there.

---

## 1. Centaurs — the biggest empty space in the scene

**Between Jupiter and Neptune, 5 to 30 AU. Nothing is drawn there at all.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Known | Roughly **450–500** catalogued |
| Estimated | About **44,000** larger than 1 km |
| Lifetime | A few million years — astronomically, no time at all |

Centaurs are the transition stage. They start as Kuiper Belt objects, get
pulled inward, cross the giant planets' orbits on unstable paths, and end up
either ejected or captured into the inner system as short-period comets. They
are half asteroid and half comet and several are observed doing both things at
once.

**Worth modelling individually:**

- **10199 Chariklo** — the largest Centaur, about 250 km, and in 2013 the
  **first small body ever found to have rings**. Two of them, at 391 and 405
  km, found when it passed in front of a star. Nobody expected an object that
  small to hold a ring system.
- **2060 Chiron** — found in 1977 and the reason the class exists. It has both
  an asteroid number and a comet designation, because it does both. It also
  appears to have rings, and unlike Chariklo's they **change**.
- **5145 Pholus** — one of the reddest objects known anywhere.
- **60558 Echeclus** — caught in a major outburst in 2005, shedding a fragment.
- **Nessus, Asbolus, Bienor, Hylonome** — the rest of the named set.

**Why this is the strongest next build:** a real population in a genuinely
empty part of the scene, plus a ringed body that is not a planet — which the
project already has the ring machinery for.

## 2. Near-Earth objects

| | |
|---|---|
| Known | Over **40,000** detected since 1990 |
| Potentially hazardous | Roughly **2,400** |
| Larger than 1 km | About **870** — nearly all of these are now found |

Four orbital families, and they are defined by geometry rather than
composition:

- **Atira** — orbits entirely inside Earth's
- **Aten** — cross Earth's orbit, spend most of their time inside it
- **Apollo** — cross Earth's orbit, spend most of their time outside it
- **Amor** — approach but never cross

**Worth modelling individually:**

- **99942 Apophis** — passes within **31,600 km** of Earth on 13 April 2029,
  closer than the geostationary satellites, and naked-eye visible from Europe
  and Africa
- **101955 Bennu** — OSIRIS-REx brought a sample back in 2023
- **162173 Ryugu** — Hayabusa2 did the same in 2020
- **433 Eros** — the first asteroid orbited and landed on, by NEAR in 2001
- **65803 Didymos / Dimorphos** — DART deliberately hit Dimorphos in 2022 and
  measurably changed its orbit. The only time humans have moved a celestial
  body on purpose.

## 3. Comets as visitable bodies

| | |
|---|---|
| Known | About **4,000** |
| Found by SOHO alone | Over **4,000**, most of them Kreutz sungrazers |

Currently there is one comet *event* (the sungrazer) and no comet you can go
and look at. A comet as a body needs a nucleus, a coma, and **two** tails that
point in different directions — the dust tail curving along the orbit, the ion
tail pointing straight away from the Sun.

Three families, by where they come from and how long they take:

- **Jupiter-family** — under 20 years, from the scattered disk
- **Halley-type** — 20 to 200 years
- **Long-period** — over 200 years, from the Oort Cloud

**Worth modelling individually:**

- **1P/Halley** — 76 years, and the reason anyone knew comets return
- **C/1995 O1 Hale-Bopp** — visible to the naked eye for 18 months, a record,
  and one of the objects whose traced-back orbit is evidence for the Oort Cloud
- **67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko** — Rosetta orbited it and Philae landed
- **C/2020 F3 NEOWISE** — the best naked-eye comet in decades
- **D/1993 F2 Shoemaker–Levy 9** — already the top entry on the events backlog

## 4. Trojans

Objects trapped 60° ahead of and behind a planet, at the L4 and L5 points.

| Planet | Known |
|---|---|
| **Jupiter** | Over **12,000** catalogued · an estimated 600,000 larger than 1 km |
| **Neptune** | About **35** |
| **Mars** | About **14** |
| **Earth** | **2** — 2010 TK7 and 2020 XL5 |
| **Uranus** | **2** |
| **Venus** | **1** |

Jupiter's two swarms are already in the scene. Nothing else is. **Neptune's
are the interesting omission** — there are far more of them than the 35 found
suggests, they are hard to see because they are dark and distant, and they are
redder than Jupiter's, which says something about where they formed.

## 5. Interstellar visitors — exactly three, ever

Objects from another star, passing through on hyperbolic paths that will never
return.

- **1I/ʻOumuamua** (2017) — elongated, tumbling, no visible tail, and it
  accelerated on the way out in a way still not fully explained
- **2I/Borisov** (2019) — unmistakably a comet, and chemically much like our own
- **3I/ATLAS** (2025) — the third, the largest, and the best observed; it has
  now left the inner Solar System

The bridge to the next project. They are not Solar System members and the
copy must not treat them as such — they are passing through.

## 6. More dwarf-planet candidates — ~~mostly done~~

**Eight are now in the scene:** Orcus, Quaoar, Gonggong, Sedna, and the four
strongest that were missing — **Salacia** (846 km, albedo 0.042, moon Actaea),
**Varda** (750 km, moon Ilmarë), **Varuna** (678 km, deformed by a 6.34-hour
spin) and **Ixion** (617 km, a Plutino).

Still a selection rather than the set: roughly **10 more** are very likely and
over **100** possible depending on the size threshold used. The next tier —
**Huya · Chaos · 2002 AW197 · Dziewanna** — is smaller, less certain, and well
into diminishing returns. The point that the IAU line is administrative rather
than physical is already made by the eight.

## 7. Structures rather than populations

- **The heliosphere** — termination shock (~85 AU), heliosheath, heliopause
  (~120 AU), heliotail. Nested bubbles, and the only Solar System boundary two
  spacecraft have physically crossed.
- **The Oort Cloud** — 5,000 to 100,000 AU, spherical, never observed.
- **Kirkwood gaps and asteroid families** — the main belt is drawn as a field
  rather than as a structured population with resonance gaps and collisional
  families (Vesta, Flora, Koronis, Eos).
- **Magnetospheres and radiation belts** — see `not-covered.md` § B; these emit
  no light at any wavelength and can only ever be diagrams.
