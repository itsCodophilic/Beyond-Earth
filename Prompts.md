Supernova and Kilonova and other types like below 
In astronomy, the term "nova" refers to a cosmic explosion occurring on the surface of a white dwarf star in a binary system, which temporarily increases the star's brightness by thousands of times. Over the years, astronomers have classified these surface flares—and extended the terminology to entirely different, vastly more powerful stellar explosions—into several distinct types.

1. True Novae (Surface Flare-ups)

These occur in binary systems where a white dwarf siphons hydrogen gas from a companion star until the pressure and temperature ignite a runaway nuclear fusion reaction on its surface. Crucially, the star survives the explosion.
• Classical Novae: The most common type. These are one-off explosions (within human timescales) that eventually fade back to their original state over weeks or months.
• Recurrent Novae: Systems where the white dwarf experiences multiple eruptions. These cycles usually repeat every few decades, such as the well-known NASA Science profile of T Coronae Borealis.
• Micronovae: Extremely small, localized thermonuclear bursts that happen at the magnetic poles of certain white dwarfs, lasting only a few hours.
• Symbiotic Novae (NC / Very Slow): Exceptionally slow eruptions that stay at peak brightness for a decade or more before slowly fading away.

2. Supernovae (Stellar Destruction)

Unlike a true nova, a supernova is a catastrophic, final explosion that completely destroys the star, leaving behind only a remnant like a neutron star or a black hole.
• Type Ia Supernovae: This happens when a white dwarf in a binary system accretes too much mass and hits the critical mass limit (1.44 solar masses). A runaway thermonuclear explosion completely obliterates the star.
• Core-Collapse Supernovae (Types II, Ib, Ic): The violent death of a single massive star (at least 8 times the mass of our sun). When it runs out of fuel, the core collapses under its own gravity and rebounds into a massive explosion.

3. Cosmic Relatives (Other "Nova" Classes)

Astronomy also uses the "nova" suffix to classify entirely different types of intense cosmic events:
• Kilonovae: A rapid, highly energetic explosion caused by the collision and merger of two neutron stars (or a neutron star and a black hole). They are famous for producing heavy elements like gold and platinum.
• Hypernovae: Also known as a superluminous supernova, these are massive core-collapse explosions from rapidly rotating stars that are 10 to 100 times brighter than a standard supernova.
• Luminous Red Novae: A distinct type of explosion caused by the direct collision and merging of two normal stars rather than a white dwarf flare
